# T5.1: Segment Selection Memo

**Date:** July 2026
**Project:** Blinkit Category Exploration
**Author:** Product Manager, Growth

## 1. Primary Target Segment
**Segment Name:** Established Routine Shoppers (Urban Millennials & Gen Z, primarily Metro)

### Definition
- **Usage Frequency:** High (orders ≥3 times a month).
- **Current Behavior:** Almost exclusively purchases staples, groceries, and instant snacks (the "Habit Loop").
- **Exploration Behavior:** Has rarely or never purchased from long-tail categories (e.g., Electronics, Pet Care, Personal Care, Toys).

## 2. Rationale (Hypothesis Scorecard Alignment)
Based on early signals and hypothesis mapping:
- **H1 (Habit Loop):** This segment is heavily entrenched. Their muscle memory dictates opening Blinkit *only* for immediate grocery needs.
- **H2 (Low Awareness) & H4 (Discovery Friction):** Because they speed-run their usual reorder lists, they do not browse the home page banners or categories, meaning they are completely blind to catalog expansion.
- **Business Impact:** This segment represents the highest volume of MACs (Monthly Active Customers). Unlocking even a 10% cross-category trial rate here will significantly move the north-star metric (incremental AOV and margin expansion).

## 3. Early Segment-Size Sanity Check
- **Market Data:** Quick commerce in India is driven by the 18–35 urban demographic. Repeat grocery shoppers form the core of the ~15M MACs across quick commerce platforms.
- **Viability:** This segment is vastly large enough to support a product intervention. It is the defining user base of the platform. If we solve discovery for them, we solve it at scale.

## 4. Exclusion Criteria
- **Churned/Dormant users:** We are solving a discovery problem, not a core retention/resurrection problem.
- **Power Explorers:** Users who already buy electronics or cosmetics on Blinkit (we want to learn from them later, but our primary intervention targets those who *don't*).
